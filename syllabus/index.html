What this course is

Turning data into decisions — from a manager's chair

This course studies information systems and business intelligence as two of the most important levers a modern organization has for turning data into decisions and advantage. We look at both from a manager's point of view: not how the technology is built, but why it matters, where it creates value, and how leaders put it to work.

From there we move into the components that make these systems run — hardware, software, data, people, and process — and see how they play out in practice through in-class exercises and case studies.

Prerequisite: 45 credit hours minimum, or consent of instructor.  ·  Consistent with Berry's mission to educate the head, heart, and hands: rigorous concepts (head), hands-on work with real cases and data (hands), and the responsible use of information as central to good leadership (heart).

The rules, in plain language

Policies worth knowing

Tap to expand. Then test yourself in the Syllabus Challenge right below.

Rules vary by assessment. Homework and cases: 10% off per day for up to 3 days, then not accepted. Quizzes and the final exam can't be submitted late — a miss is a zero. Case (group) deadlines don't move for one member's conflict; the group redistributes or works ahead. The way to protect yourself is always the same: if you can foresee a conflict, tell me in advance.
Know about a conflict ahead of time (athletics, an interview, a religious observance)? Tell me at least one week in advance and we'll arrange alternative timing. Quizzes are in class, so a conflict means a make-up sitting — ordinarily taken before the conflict — with documentation.
You start with full credit and keep it with two or fewer absences plus active participation. Each absence beyond two costs 5 points. Only full, in-person sessions count, and arriving more than 10 minutes late is an absence. Give me a heads-up when you'll miss.
Groups (~4–5) form early and stay together for all three case analyses. Peer evaluation covers the 60 case points: half comes from the group's score, half is scaled by your peer rating (capped at 100%). A rating below 80% needs a short written reason. Not submitting your peer evaluation costs 5 points. Raise contribution problems early — not for the first time in December.
Acceptable: working together outside class on the case studies; asking me clarifying questions anytime. Not acceptable: any collaboration on a Canvas quiz; getting a solution from online sources or a past student. When unsure, just ask. All violations are reported to the Office of the Provost.
Generative AI is a thinking aid, not a substitute for your own analysis or writing, and every use must be disclosed. On the case analyses you may use it to brainstorm, test understanding, or get feedback on work you've written. It may not write your analysis, and it's prohibited entirely on quizzes and the final exam. Undisclosed AI use is treated as academic dishonesty.
Every assignment counts — nothing is dropped. Quizzes and the final exam are each taken once. Homework may be resubmitted until its deadline (the latest submission is graded). Your Canvas running total isn't your course grade — the point totals in the syllabus are.

How class runs

Built on a weekly rhythm

This course is highly interactive — short lectures, then discussion, cases, and exercises. Staying in sync with the weekly loop is the single best predictor of success. Plan on ~2.5 hours in class and 5–7.5 hours of focused work outside it each week.

STEP 1
Prepare
Read the assigned material and finish any prior work before class.
STEP 2
Engage
Show up on time, join the discussion, work the cases with your group.
STEP 3
Apply
Quizzes and homework check what you practiced; the cases build your analysis skills.
STEP 4
Review & read ahead
Revisit the slides, then read into the next session so you arrive ready.
